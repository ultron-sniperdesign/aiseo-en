**Performance in Google AI Mode can't be isolated from the data you can actually get.** The Search Console report shows impressions for AI Overviews and AI Mode together, traffic from assistants blends together in analytics, and a manual test shows you a single answer. What you can measure is the total and its direction, not the performance of one surface.

This article isn't a tool walkthrough — those already exist. It's about what the numbers you collect actually support, so no claim ends up in your report that won't hold up.

## Why the total is a weak proxy

It's tempting to treat the combined number as "close enough to AI Mode." But it merges two surfaces we know behave differently.

In an Ahrefs analysis of US data from September 2025 that captures individual generations, the cited URLs of AI Overviews and AI Mode overlapped by **just 13.7 percent**. [AI Mode vs. AI Overviews](/blog/ai-mode-vs-ai-overviews/) goes through it in detail.

<Insight tone="aio">
When the combined number goes up, it can be growth in one surface, the other, or both — **and the number won't tell you which**. So the total isn't a reliable proxy for AI Mode. It's the sum of two things that are documented to diverge.
</Insight>

## Common mistakes

<MistakeGrid>
  <Mistake num="01" title="Passing the total off as AI Mode">
    The most common one. The report merges AI Overviews and AI Mode into a single view, so you can't read one surface's performance out of it.
  </Mistake>
  <Mistake num="02" title="Reading impressions as traffic">
    The report gives you impressions, not clicks and not CTR. An impression is a signal that you showed up, not a visit.
  </Mistake>
  <Mistake num="03" title="Drawing a conclusion from one run">
    Source selection fluctuates between runs. One answer you tried is an anecdote, not a measurement.
  </Mistake>
  <Mistake num="04" title="Mixing Google AI with other assistants">
    The Search Console report covers Google's generative features only. ChatGPT and Perplexity aren't in it.
  </Mistake>
</MistakeGrid>

<Insight tone="warn">
There's a good reason to repeat your measurements: earlier research cited in the Ahrefs study puts it at roughly **45 percent of citations changing between individual generations**. A one-off check captures a moment rather than a state — it only starts to mean something once you repeat it over time and across more queries.
</Insight>

## What this looks like on real data

Our own store's measurement shows the limit clearly. We recorded **200,000 impressions** in Google's generative AI features in under two months — but that number doesn't separate the surfaces. And **1,867 visits** from AI tools over twelve months, **90 percent** of them sent by ChatGPT — a tool that isn't in Search Console at all.

Both are useful numbers. Neither one says anything about how the site is doing specifically in AI Mode. The full data is in the [case study](/blog/case-study-ai-traffic/), and we ran into the same limit in [AI Mode for e-commerce](/blog/ai-mode-for-ecommerce/).

## Key takeaways

As of publication, AI Mode can't be isolated from the data you can actually get. Search Console merges it with AI Overviews, analytics can't tell the surfaces apart, and a manual test shows a moment rather than volume.

That doesn't mean measuring blind. The total and its trend are a usable signal — you just have to say that it's a total. The most valuable skill in reporting AI visibility today is telling what was measured apart from what we filled in around it.

And when someone offers guaranteed, measurable AI Mode performance, it's worth asking what data they're reading it from.
