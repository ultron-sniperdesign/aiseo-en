**AI Overviews is a summary at the top of a regular results page, with the classic links still below it. AI Mode is a standalone conversational mode where the answer stands on its own and you can keep asking follow-ups.** Both build an answer from multiple sources, but they often cite different URLs — so you can't treat them as one surface.

From the outside it looks like one thing: "Google answers questions itself now." In practice these are two different places with different behavior — and that has a fairly concrete consequence for how you measure and what you can promise.

The common denominator matters more than it looks: based on what we've seen so far, both break the query into sub-questions and assemble the answer from several sources. How that works is covered in the piece on [query fan-out](/blog/query-fan-out/).

## Same answer, different sources

This is the most interesting finding — and also where you need to be careful with the numbers.

Working from US data collected in September 2025 that captures individual generations, Ahrefs compared answers from both surfaces across a large sample: roughly 730,000 answer pairs for content similarity and 540,000 query pairs for citations. The answers were close in meaning — average semantic similarity around 86 percent, while word-level overlap came in around 16 percent. **The cited URLs overlapped by 13.7 percent**, and by 16.3 percent for the top three citations. In other words: both said much the same thing, but they backed it with different pages.

<Insight tone="aio">
The practical implication is simple: **a citation in one surface doesn't guarantee a citation in the other.** If you confirm that the summary above the results mentions you, you've learned nothing about AI Mode — and vice versa.
</Insight>

Before you file that number away as fact, it comes with three caveats — either flagged by the study itself or baked into how it was set up.

<Insight tone="warn">
**This was US data from September 2025** — one market, and not current. **It was a snapshot of individual generations**, not a picture of source sets over time. And earlier research the study references found that roughly 45 percent of citations in AI Overviews change between generations — so the real overlap in sources may be higher than the snapshot suggests.
</Insight>

The conclusion you can safely draw from the study is more sober than the headline number: even where more than one source could support an answer, the two surfaces often reach for different ones.

## What this changes for your site

**Measure them separately.** Finding yourself in one tells you nothing about the other. How to check your visibility is covered in the [AI visibility test](/blog/ai-visibility-test/); for Search Console data, see [the AI report in GSC](/blog/gsc-ai-report/).

**One run isn't enough.** When source selection shifts between runs, a single test is an anecdote. Repeat it over time and across more queries.

**But the optimization doesn't split in two.** The foundation is the same for both: answer a specific sub-question clearly, keep your sources verifiable, and keep your data consistent. The practical content prep is covered in [Google AI Mode](/blog/google-ai-mode-content/).

## What to take away

AI Overviews and AI Mode blur easily into a single "Google answers questions itself now." But the difference is practical: the first is a summary above the results that leaves the links in place below it, the second is a standalone conversation without them.

And above all — the available analysis suggests that even when they say similar things, they often back them with different sources. So track them separately, don't take one measurement as proof, and with numbers from elsewhere, always ask which market they came from and when.
