**According to available 2026 analyses, LinkedIn is one of the most frequently cited sources in AI search — on professional and B2B queries it tends to be among the most-cited sites across ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Google AI Mode.** Getting cited from it takes more than having a profile: original expert content, consistency, and direct answers to customer questions all help. This guide covers what AI actually cites from LinkedIn and how to work on it.

## Why LinkedIn is so strong for AI

LinkedIn is a large source of expert content with clear author identity — exactly the signals AI platforms reward when they pick sources. According to available 2026 marketing analyses, LinkedIn comes out in some samples as the second most-cited site in AI answers (right behind Reddit), and on professional queries it climbs even higher.

Its weight also appears to be growing. Publicly available analyses from the turn of 2025 into 2026 show LinkedIn's share of citations rising in the samples they track, especially on ChatGPT. How AI picks sources in general is covered in our article on [how AI cites sources](/blog/how-ai-cites-sources/).

<Insight tone="aio">
LinkedIn is an off-site channel where you have a lot of control over what you publish and how often. Unlike Reddit, where other people do most of the writing about you, here you write it yourself — and that makes it one of the few levers on AI visibility that you genuinely control.
</Insight>

## The most common mistakes

  <Mistake num="01" title="Only resharing other people's content">

  </Mistake>
  <Mistake num="02" title="Chasing likes instead of relevance">
    Cited posts often don't have much engagement, according to available analyses. Optimizing for viral reach instead of a substantive answer can miss what AI more often selects on.

  <Mistake num="03" title="Publishing in bursts">
    Without consistency, the odds of a citation tend to be lower. Most cited authors are frequent publishers, not people posting once a quarter.

## Key takeaways

- **LinkedIn is among the most-cited sources in AI** — on professional and B2B queries it's one of the most common, according to 2026 analyses.
- **Original expert articles dominate** (on the order of 500–2,000 words); reshares get cited far less often.
- **Run both the company page and personal profiles** — the platforms differ in what they prefer.
- **Relevance and expertise count for more than reach** — smaller accounts get cited too.
- **Consistency helps** — several posts a month across the team.
- **LinkedIn is an off-site channel you control** — verify the results with an [AI visibility test](/blog/ai-visibility-test/).
