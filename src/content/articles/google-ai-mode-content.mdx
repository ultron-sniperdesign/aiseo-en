## Google AI Mode: what it is and why it matters

**Google AI Mode is a conversational search mode built on the Gemini family of models that puts an AI answer assembled from multiple sources front and center, instead of a classic list of links.** It doesn't work one query at a time with one result — it holds a conversation, answering follow-ups in the context of what came before. According to the information available, it has been rolling out market by market since 2025, so it's no longer just a US experiment. Availability can still vary by account, device, and region.

This guide covers what makes AI Mode different from AI Overviews, how **query fan-out** works, and what to actually do with your content so Google has a better shot at using and citing it in AI answers. It builds on the platform guides [SEO for ChatGPT](/blog/chatgpt-seo/) and [optimizing for Perplexity](/blog/perplexity-seo/) — together they cover the three surfaces where AI visibility is decided today.

## AI Mode vs. AI Overviews: what's the difference

People blur the two terms, but they're two different things. **AI Overviews** is a summary box above the classic results — the blue links stay below it. **AI Mode** is a standalone conversational interface where the answer stands on its own and you can keep asking.

The practical consequence: treat them as one target and you'll easily end up with content that works well for neither. Available analyses also suggest that while the two surfaces say broadly similar things, they often cite different sources. For a detailed comparison, including the data and its limits, see [AI Mode vs. AI Overviews](/blog/ai-mode-vs-ai-overviews/).

## How query fan-out works

The key to AI Mode is a technique called **query fan-out**. Google describes it as breaking a query into related sub-queries: instead of one answer to one query, you get **several parallel sub-questions**, each searched separately. The final answer is then assembled from passages across different sites.

- It often works with specific **excerpts** and source links rather than a rating of the whole page — which is exactly why modularity matters so much.
- The passage it uses doesn't have to come from a page that ranks high in the classic results — for one sub-question, Google may pick a different relevant passage.
- It pays to cover a topic **in depth**, in self-contained, well-targeted sections that answer the individual questions.

The [robots.txt guide for AI](/blog/robots-txt-for-ai-crawlers/) covers how to technically open your site to search crawlers and AI systems.

## Step by step: how to prepare content for AI Mode

The core isn't a trick — it's structure and substance: keep the page accessible and eligible for snippets, keep the substance in the text, write in passages that make sense on their own, and back up what you claim. Google's own documentation states that no special requirements or separate markup for AI features are needed.

The full step-by-step process, including what Google explicitly doesn't require, is covered in [how to appear in AI Mode](/blog/how-to-appear-in-ai-mode/).

## AI Mode rollout: what it changes right now

While AI Mode was US-only, it was a distant trend. According to the information available, it has been reaching more markets and languages since 2025, so it's starting to matter for a lot more sites. Availability can still vary by account, device, and region:

- It usually shows up as a **separate tab** in Search and in the Google app.
- For some queries, users get their answer right in the interface and **click through less often** — so track the impact in Search Console.
- A citation in an AI answer has value even without a click: it builds **brand and trust**. Measure the indirect payoff too, not just visit volume.

If you're going the other way and want to **limit** your presence in AI Overviews, there's a separate guide on [how to turn off AI Overviews](/blog/how-to-turn-off-ai-overviews/).
