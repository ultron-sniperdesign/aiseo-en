  - q: "Why use a 301 instead of a 302?"
    a: "A 301 redirect signals a permanent change; a 302 signals a temporary one. A migration is permanent, so a 301 tells search engines clearly that the new URL replaces the old one. A temporary 302 can leave systems holding on to the original address, which drags out the ambiguity for no reason."
  - q: "Can I delete the old URLs eventually?"
    a: "It's better to leave the redirects running long term. AI systems ingested your content earlier and can point at an old URL well after the fact. As long as the redirect works, the visitor lands on the right page. Kill the URL and that citation becomes a link to a broken page."
  - q: "How fast does a migration show up in AI answers?"
    a: "Expect a lag. Regular search usually processes changes relatively quickly, but with some AI tools the changes can surface later, and the pace varies from tool to tool. So for a while, AI may keep pointing at your old URLs. That's one more reason to keep the redirects working."
  - q: "What should I check right after launch?"
    a: "Start with robots.txt, noindex tags, and canonical URLs, because a crawl block, an indexing block, or a pointer to the staging domain often survives the move.     a: "You can, but you make evaluation harder on yourself. When URLs, templates, and copy all change at once, you won't be able to tell what caused a drop if one happens. **A site migration or redesign is a change to your URLs, templates, or domain that risks losing indexing, rankings, and citations in AI answers.** The essentials are a map of old URLs to new ones, permanent 301 redirects, structured data carried over intact, and measurement both before and after launch. This guide covers what to watch before, during, and after a migration — including confirming that old URLs still lead to matching content and that the new build hasn't blocked crawling or indexing.

## What migrations add in the AI era

Classic SEO migration work watches indexing and rankings: redirects, sitemaps, internal links. That still holds. But there's a new layer on top.

Many AI tools cite **specific URLs** or source pages in their answers. When a URL changes and the old one stops working, the citation loses its destination. On top of that, these tools ingested your content earlier and refresh on **their own schedule** — with some of them, changes surface later than they do in regular search, and the pace varies from tool to tool. So for a while, AI may point at URLs you no longer have — and whether the visitor lands on the right page or on an error comes down to your redirects.

## After launch: what to measure

The first few days matter. Track:

- **Indexing and crawl errors** — especially a spike in 404s, which points to missing redirects.
- **Internal links and navigation** — make sure they point straight to the new URLs, not through a redirect.
- **AI traffic** — if you track it (through custom segments or traffic sources), check whether it dropped; [AI segments in Search Console](/blog/gsc-ai-report/) help here.
- **AI visibility, manually** — run your key queries through a [visibility test](/blog/ai-visibility-test/) and compare against the pre-migration picture.
- **Structured data** — whether the new template actually outputs it; see [structured data for AI](/blog/structured-data-for-ai/).

<Insight tone="warning">
Don't panic over a drop in the first few days. Short-term swings after a migration are normal, and if the migration is technically sound, visibility can come back gradually. What should get your attention is 404s climbing or pages dropping out of the index in Search Console — those aren't signs of things settling, they're signs of a missing redirect.
</Insight>

## Common mistakes

<MistakeGrid>
  <Mistake number="01" title="A 404 instead of a redirect">
    The fastest way to lose both your rankings and the foundation for AI citations. Every old URL needs a destination.
  </Mistake>
  <Mistake number="02" title="Everything to the homepage">
    Redirecting old URLs to the homepage en masse can be treated as a soft 404 or an irrelevant redirect, and the relevance is lost.
  </Mistake>
  <Mistake number="03" title="A forgotten crawl block">
    A line blocking crawling of the whole site survives from staging. The site is live for users, but robots.txt can stop search crawlers from crawling it.
  </Mistake>
  <Mistake number="04" title="Everything at once">
    Changing URLs, templates, and copy together means you won't know what caused a drop. Separate the steps when you can.
  </Mistake>
</MistakeGrid>

## Key takeaways

- **A migration changes what search engines and AI already know** — without a URL map, you risk losing indexing and citations alike.
- **301s and a single hop** — permanent redirects, no chains, no blanket redirect to the homepage.
- **Leave the redirects running for a long time** — AI can point at old URLs well after the fact.
- **Structured data and robots.txt** — the two things a new template breaks most often.
- **Measure before and after** — without a baseline you can't evaluate a drop; [why URLs matter](/blog/how-ai-cites-sources/).
