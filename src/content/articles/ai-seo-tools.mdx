## AI SEO tools: what they're for and how they differ

**AI SEO tools track whether and how often a brand shows up in AI answers and assistants — in Perplexity, ChatGPT with web search, or in Google AI Overviews inside Google's results.** They differ from classic SEO tools in what they measure: instead of rankings and links, they track **share of mentions (share of voice), the sources cited where those are shown, and competitors**.

This overview isn't a "best tool" leaderboard — the right tool depends on the size of your site and your budget. It reflects the state of play as of May 2026; tool pricing and features change fast. We'll go through what these tools measure, what the tiers look like from free to enterprise, and when paying starts to pay off. Classic tools get [their own overview](/blog/seo-tools/).

## What AI SEO tools measure

These are the features you'll run into most often with paid tools; the scope varies by tool and plan:

- **Share of mentions (share of voice)** — put simply, how often you show up versus your competitors across a tracked set of queries; the exact calculation differs from tool to tool.
- **Citations / sources** — where a platform lists its sources, tools track the specific URLs used in the answer.
- **Change over time and alerts** — some tools chart shifts in share of mentions and flag significant swings.
- **Context and sentiment** — select tools assess the context a brand appears in; methodologies vary.

This builds on [measuring AI visibility as part of an audit](/blog/ai-seo-audit/) — a tool just automates what you can do by hand at the start.

## Three tiers of tools

### 1. Free and manual

To get started, and for a small site, a directional manual check is usually enough: run a small set of customer queries through ChatGPT and Perplexity and watch for whether your brand shows up. GA4 can fill in the picture with visits from AI platforms, but it won't capture mentions that never get clicked — treat it as a supporting signal. Take the result as a first read, not precise measurement.

### 2. Entry-level trackers

Paid tools automatically run sets of queries across AI platforms and calculate share of mentions. Examples: **Otterly**, **Peec**, and others; entry-level tools typically start in the low tens of dollars a month (Otterly Lite runs $29/mo). Pricing and features both move fast — verify the exact number against the current pricing page.

### 3. For larger companies (enterprise)

Larger companies and agencies have access to more robust platforms for competitive benchmarking, working across a bigger set of topics, and sometimes query demand estimates — for the measurement layer specifically, **Profound** (typically $500+/mo) or **LLM Pulse**. The price is an order of magnitude higher, and it only makes sense once you're tracking topics at real volume.

## Check coverage for your market

Most trackers are built around English-language queries first. If you operate in a non-English market, a narrow niche, or a region where local competitors dominate, coverage of your queries varies from tool to tool — verify it on your own key queries before you commit to an annual plan. If you already pay for a classic SEO tool, check whether AI tracking is bundled in (Ahrefs Brand Radar, Semrush One AI) before you buy a second subscription.

## When to start paying

A quick look at the tiers:

| Tier | Examples | Who it's for | What to verify |
|---|---|---|---|
| Free and manual | ChatGPT, Perplexity, GA4 as a supplement | small site, first read | track the same queries and save the results |
| Entry-level tools | Otterly, Peec | dozens of queries, competitors | language support, platforms, exports |
| Enterprise platforms | Profound, LLM Pulse | agencies, larger brands | price, calculation methodology, market coverage |
| Bundled add-ons | Ahrefs Brand Radar, Semrush One AI | teams already paying for a classic tool | how deep the AI tracking actually goes |

The order is simple: **measure manually first**, and move to a paid tool only once that outgrows you.

- A handful of queries and a small site → free manual measurement.
- Dozens of queries, competitive tracking, a need for alerts → an entry-level tracker.
- A large volume of topics, agency reporting → an enterprise platform.

Investing in a tool makes sense from the point where a manual audit takes more than a few dozen minutes a month.
