**In Google's view, AEO and GEO are part of SEO. Google laid this out in its first official guide to optimizing for generative AI search, published on May 15, 2026 — both AI Overviews and AI Mode draw on the same Search index and comparable quality signals as classic results.** Google explicitly writes that you don't need new special files or special structured data types “just for AI.” This article sums up what the Google guide actually says, where it's right, and where its position is simplified relative to the broader AI ecosystem.

## What Google published on May 15, 2026

The document titled **“Optimizing your website for generative AI features on Google Search”** sits in a new **“Generative AI fundamentals”** section of the Search Central documentation. It was published on May 15, 2026. The exact wording can change over time, so we recommend watching for later updates directly in the official documentation.

It's available at `developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide`. Google also published a companion post on the Google Search Central Blog.

<Insight tone="aio">
The guide's core message: you don't need a separate new discipline for Google AI Overviews and AI Mode. The same Search index and comparable quality signals that drive classic organic results also play an important role in how Google picks sources for its AI answers.
</Insight>

## What Google explicitly says

### From Google's perspective, AEO and GEO are still SEO

Google writes openly in the guide: *“AEO stands for answer engine optimization and GEO for generative engine optimization. These are both terms you may see used to describe work specifically focused on improving visibility in AI search experiences. From Google Search's perspective, optimizing for generative AI search is optimizing for the search experience, and thus still SEO.”*

Asked whether SEO is still relevant for generative AI search, Google answers: **“In short, yes!”** Its argument is that *“the same index and quality signals that earn rankings are what earn citations in AI answers.”*

### What Google says you DO need

To appear as a **supporting link** in AI Overviews or AI Mode, Google names three **technical prerequisites** as the baseline:

1. **Being indexed** in the standard Google Search index.
2. **Being eligible to appear with a snippet** (no `noindex` or `nosnippet` in your directives, for example).
3. **Meeting the general Google Search technical requirements** — indexability, availability, mobile rendering, HTTPS.

On the content side, Google recommends:

- **Unique, valuable content** with a point of view of its own (not other people's information retold).
- **A people-first approach** (people-first content, the principles of the Helpful Content Update).
- **Clear organization** — obvious headings, images, video, easy navigation.

<Insight tone="warn">
The technical conditions read as trivial, but in reality they rule out plenty of sites: a site with broken HTTPS, slow on mobile, with snippets blocked via `nosnippet`, or poorly indexed in Google Search stays out of AI Overviews no matter how good the content is.
</Insight>

## How Google AI Overviews work technically

The guide describes a process in which the AI answer is built from sources retrieved in the Search index and then assembled into a response. The technical literature calls this principle RAG (retrieval-augmented generation), though Google doesn't necessarily present that term as settled in its own guide.

**What that means in practice for site owners:**

- If a page **performs well in ordinary organic results** in Google Search, it has a real chance of showing up in AI Overviews too.
- **Freshness, authority, technical health** = the same kind of quality signals as for classic results.
- **This isn't a separate index.** AI Overviews and AI Mode are built on top of the standard Search index — source selection follows on from Google's ordinary search infrastructure, but you can't reduce it to simply lifting the classic ranking order.

## Where Google is right — and where its position is simplified

Google is in the position of **a company describing its own search engine**. Its guide applies to Google AI Overviews and AI Mode. But the AI ecosystem is broader, and by definition Google doesn't speak for its competitors.

## What to take away

- **On May 15, 2026, Google published its first official guide** to generative AI search.
- **Google says AEO and GEO are still SEO** — its AI Overviews and AI Mode draw on the same Search index and the same quality signals.
- **You don't need** new special files (no “AI text file”), special structured data, or a special way of writing purely for AI.
- **You do need** a page that's indexed, snippet-eligible, and meets the Search technical requirements — plus unique content and clear organization.
- **The Google guide applies to Google.** For citations in ChatGPT, Perplexity, Claude, and Gemini, the AI ecosystem is broader, and AEO/GEO practice (llms.txt, short chunks, brand mentions) may help.
- **The pragmatic approach:** solid SEO plus supplementary AEO/GEO tactics for AI systems outside Google. Not one at the expense of the other.
