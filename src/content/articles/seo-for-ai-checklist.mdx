## SEO for AI: where to start and what to tackle in what order

**SEO for AI is a practical process for preparing your site so that AI tools and traditional search engines can find it, understand it, and potentially cite it.** This article isn't another conceptual overview — you'll find that in the pillar guide [SEO vs GEO vs AEO](/seo-vs-geo-vs-aeo/). This one is a **checklist: what to do, and in what order.**

The good news up front: you don't need two separate strategies. One well-structured, technically healthy, credible site is the shared foundation for Google and for tools like ChatGPT and Perplexity. The AI layer just goes on top of it.

## SEO for AI isn't a second SEO

Traditional SEO and optimizing for AI overlap heavily: technical quality, a clear structure, readable content, and credibility help both. The main difference is what you're additionally aiming at — citations in AI answers alongside rankings. [SEO vs GEO](/blog/seo-vs-geo/) breaks down when each layer carries more weight.

<Insight tone="accent" icon="!" label="The right order">
  Work **technical → content → measurement**. Until AI can find you, there's no point tuning content; until you're measuring, you won't know what works.
</Insight>

## Step 1: Measure your starting point

Without a starting point, you won't know whether your changes are helping. Run a simple [AI SEO audit](/blog/ai-seo-audit/):

- Run 15–20 customer queries through ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Google; for each one, save the exact wording, the date, the tool, and a screenshot of the answer (ideally with personalization off).
- Check your queries, rankings, and traffic in Google Search Console.
- Save the result as a baseline you can come back to.

## Step 2: Open your site to AI search crawlers and Bing

Before you touch content, confirm that AI can find you at all:

- In your `robots.txt`, let the AI search crawlers through (for example OAI-SearchBot, PerplexityBot, and the relevant Anthropic user agents for Claude — per their current documentation); more details are in the guide to [SEO for ChatGPT](/blog/chatgpt-seo/).
- Verify your site in **Bing Webmaster Tools** — Bing is a practical discoverability check, and it tends to be an important source for part of AI search.
- If you'd rather limit how your content gets used, distinguish between AI search crawlers and Google; with Google's AI Overviews, the options for limiting them separately aren't the same as blocking AI search crawlers, and they can affect regular results too — the [guide to limiting AI Overviews](/blog/how-to-turn-off-ai-overviews/) covers that.

## Step 3: Implement structured data

Structured data (one of the [GEO](/generative-engine-optimization/) techniques) helps machines recognize the content type, the author, and the organization:

- Across the site, focus on **Article** and **Organization**; use **FAQPage** only where the page really does have questions and answers.
- Check validity in the Schema.org validator; use the **Rich Results Test** for the types Google supports.
- Valid markup won't guarantee a citation, but it does reduce the risk that machines misread your content.

## Step 4: Write citable content

This is where it's decided whether AI has anything to cite. [AI SEO content](/blog/ai-seo-content/) covers it in depth; in short:

- **An answer block at the top** — 40–60 words that make sense on their own.
- **Fact density** — concrete numbers and examples instead of generic phrasing.
- **Evidence** — cite your sources and demonstrate experience, expertise, authoritativeness, and trustworthiness (E-E-A-T).

## Step 5: Measure and repeat

SEO for AI isn't a one-time setup:

- Track your **share of mentions** against competitors on key topics across [ChatGPT](/blog/chatgpt-seo/) and [Perplexity](/blog/perplexity-seo/).
- Review your key pages regularly; on time-sensitive topics, refresh the data and examples (freshness helps there), and on evergreen content, change only what improves accuracy.
- Run through the whole checklist again roughly every quarter.

## How long before you see results

It depends on your site's existing authority. As a working window, check technical changes after a few weeks and evaluate content changes after one to three months. It's not a guarantee of citations in AI answers, but it is a practical framework for measurement.
