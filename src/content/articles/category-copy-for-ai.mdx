  - q: "How long should category copy be?"
    a: "The intro above the product grid usually runs somewhere around 150 to 300 words — enough to explain what the category is about and how to choose within it, but not so long that it pushes the products off the screen. Longer content, like detailed buying criteria, type comparisons, or an FAQ, belongs below the grid. What matters more than length is whether the copy genuinely helps the buyer decide."
  - q: "Where should category copy go — above or below the grid?"
    a: "Both are worth doing. Put the short answer and the most important criteria above the grid, because users and AI see them right away. Put the extended content — detailed buying criteria, comparison tables, or an FAQ — below the products. Avoid hiding all the copy behind a read more link or dumping it at the very bottom with no summary up top."
  - q: "What structured data should a category page have?"

**Category copy helps AI understand your product range and the criteria buyers decide on, which can raise the odds AI uses you on queries like “best X” or “X for Y.”** On commercial queries, AI often draws on overview and category pages, not just individual products. This guide covers what to write in your category copy and how to back it with structured data, so both AI and customers get something out of it.

## Why categories matter for AI

When someone asks AI for the “best X for Y,” the model needs to understand the whole range and what to decide on within it. That is exactly what well-written category copy provides: an overview of what you sell, how you break it down, and what the buying criteria are. Product pages then fill in the details — we cover how in the article on [product pages for AI](/blog/product-pages-for-ai/).

Some 2026 analyses also find that AI answers show up often on “best [product]” queries; the share is directional and varies by industry. So on shopping topics, categories have a real shot at being one of the sources.

<Insight tone="aio">
The key shift: a category is no longer just a signpost pointing at products. To AI it is a page you can read an entire selection off of and recommend from. So it should help the buyer decide, not just list what you have in stock.
</Insight>

## The most common mistakes

<MistakeGrid>
  <Mistake num="01" title="A category with no copy">
    A bare product grid tells AI very little. With no intro copy and no criteria, the model has nothing to work from to understand what the category is about and who it suits.
  </Mistake>
  <Mistake num="02" title="Copy written for keywords only">
    Text stuffed with keywords and no value for the buyer helps neither people nor AI. Usefulness to the buyer usually does more than phrase density.
  </Mistake>
  <Mistake num="03" title="It describes but does not help you decide">
    Copy that describes the range in general terms, with no criteria and no segmentation, makes the choice no easier for the buyer or for AI. A category should help people decide.
  </Mistake>
  <Mistake num="04" title="Duplicate copy across categories">
    A generic paragraph copied into every category tends to do less for AI and search engines, and it often does not help the buyer either. Every important category deserves its own specific copy.
  </Mistake>
</MistakeGrid>

## What to take away

- **Categories can be one of AI's sources on shopping queries** — on “best X,” AI often draws on the overview, not just the products.
- **The short answer and the criteria belong above the grid** — users and AI see them right away.
- **The copy should help people decide** — criteria, segmentation, a comparison of types, not just a list.
- **Add CollectionPage, ItemList, and BreadcrumbList** — a machine-readable map of the category.
- **Every important category gets its own copy** — generic duplicate descriptions do not help.
- **Link things up and verify** the results across AI platforms; we cover the broader e-commerce context in [SEO for e-commerce in the AI era](/blog/seo-for-ecommerce-ai-era/).
