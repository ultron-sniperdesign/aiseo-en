## SEO vs. GEO: two layers, not rivals

**SEO and GEO are two layers of visibility.** SEO (Search Engine Optimization) targets rankings and clicks in classic search; GEO (Generative Engine Optimization — not geographic SEO) targets mentions and citations in the answers from AI tools with search, such as ChatGPT with search, Perplexity, or Google AI Overviews. This article is the narrower head-to-head — when to play for rankings and when to play for citations; the full four-part picture is in the [SEO vs. GEO vs. AEO guide](/seo-vs-geo-vs-aeo/).

The main idea up front: **it isn't an either/or choice.** SEO remains the foundation and GEO builds on it. So the question isn't "SEO or GEO," it's how much capacity to put into each layer.

The difference fits in one sentence: in SEO, **links** send people your way; in GEO, **citations** do.

## Why it isn't an either/or choice

GEO isn't a replacement for SEO — it's an extra layer. Two practical reasons back that up:

- **AI with search works from the discoverable web.** ChatGPT with search, Perplexity, and AI Overviews often draw on what's publicly discoverable and technically accessible. Without SEO foundations (accessibility, quality, authority), the odds of them reaching you drop sharply.
- **Rankings and citations overlap.** In practice, the overlap gets tracked often: a share of the pages cited in AI answers also ranks high in organic results. Good SEO therefore supports GEO — though it isn't a rule that holds in every industry.

Put differently: neglect SEO and you're giving GEO a weaker foundation.

## Zero-click: fewer visits, a different kind of value

GEO has one fundamental difference from SEO — its value is often **zero-click**. The user gets an answer that includes your mention, but doesn't have to click through to your site.

Traffic arriving directly from AI platforms is still low on many sites — check it in your own data. On some sites, though, it can be higher quality, because the user often shows up after researching a topic. So don't measure GEO by visit volume alone; also track whether and how AI tools mention you.

## When to invest in GEO first

GEO makes more sense wherever your audience uses AI tools to make decisions:

- **B2B and consulting services** — a longer decision cycle, where the buyer is looking for recommendations.
- **Higher-priced or specialized B2C** with a longer decision cycle — premium electronics, sporting equipment, or hobby products that people compare before buying.
- **Educational and comparison topics** — a classic entry point for an AI query.

Here a citation can put you on the shortlist you'd never reach from lower positions in Google.

## When focusing mainly on SEO is enough

On the other hand, for purely transactional and local things with no long research phase, GEO has less impact:

- **Some local services and retail**, where the user is mainly after availability, distance, or contact details — local SEO and Google Business Profile come first.
- **Impulse and routine purchases** — across part of the catalog, price, availability, or habit decide, so the research phase tends to be weaker.

Here it makes sense to keep the center of gravity on classic SEO and treat GEO as an add-on.

## What's next: where to invest on your site

SEO vs. GEO isn't a fight — it's a question of ratio. For most sites the rule holds: keep your SEO foundations healthy and add a GEO layer wherever your audience is close to AI tools.

Where that ratio sits for your site comes out of your own data — how your audience researches, whether AI tools already mention you, and how much of your demand is purely transactional. If the SEO foundations aren't in place yet, start there; the GEO layer will have far more to build on.
