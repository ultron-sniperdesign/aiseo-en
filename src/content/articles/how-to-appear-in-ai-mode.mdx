**Google AI Mode is a conversational search experience, and according to Google's documentation there are no special requirements or separate markup for it.** A page therefore has to clear the same basics as it does for regular search: be indexed, be crawlable, be eligible for snippets, keep the substance in text, and carry structured data that matches what's visible.

That sounds like an anticlimax, and deliberately so. Plenty of "special AI Mode methods" get sold out there — and the most useful thing to say about them is that, according to Google itself, none of them are needed.

## What Google actually says

Google's documentation on AI features states that appearing in AI Overviews or in AI Mode takes **no additional requirements and no special optimization**. What it lists is the usual set: keep the page indexed and eligible for snippets, allow crawling, connect your content with internal links, provide a good page experience, keep the substance in text form, and use structured data that matches the visible text.

Just as useful is what it says you **don't** need: new machine-readable files, special text files for AI, or dedicated structured data created because of AI features.

## Why passages specifically

This is the part that goes beyond the bare basics — and it isn't a trick, it's how the answer gets built. Based on the descriptions available so far, AI Mode breaks a query into sub-questions and looks for a suitable passage for each one, so the final answer is assembled from passages across several sources. [Query fan-out](/blog/query-fan-out/) covers this in detail, including the limits of what's actually known.

A few very specific things follow from that:

- **Its own heading for every sub-question.** Not "More information," but the question the passage answers.
- **The answer at the top of the passage**, the elaboration after it.
- **No "as we wrote above."** A passage pulled out of context still has to hold up.
- **Name the subject in the sentence.** Instead of "the same applies here," say what it applies to.

## When you'd rather not be used

The same documentation covers the opposite direction. How much of your page can be used in a snippet is controlled by the `nosnippet`, `data-nosnippet`, and `max-snippet` directives, or you can drop the page entirely with `noindex`. It's a blunt instrument — it limits regular results too, not just AI answers. More details in [how to limit AI Overviews](/blog/how-to-turn-off-ai-overviews/).

## The limits of this article

**It doesn't guarantee an appearance or a citation.** It describes the eligibility conditions and how passages get selected, not the outcome.

**It doesn't cover where AI Mode is available.** The rollout is gradual and varies by market, account, and rollout stage, so we'd rather not state a specific status here.

**It's based on vendor documentation.** What Google says about its own products is authoritative for Google's surfaces, but it's still a vendor source; other AI systems behave their own way.

## What to take away

The most useful thing about this question is that the answer is boring. Google states that there are no special requirements for AI Mode — so the work remains where it always was: an available page, the substance in text, claims you can back up.

In practice, that makes it worth thinking about readability at the passage level too. Not for the algorithm's sake, but because the answer usually gets built from passages — and a passage that falls apart without its surroundings is harder to use.

Then measurement: repeatedly, across several queries, and each surface separately. [The AI visibility test](/blog/ai-visibility-test/) covers what to start testing.
