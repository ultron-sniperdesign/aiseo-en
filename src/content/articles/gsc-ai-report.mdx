**On June 3, 2026, Google launched the Generative AI features report in Search Console — the first official view of how often your pages appear in AI Overviews (the AI summaries in Google Search results), AI Mode (the conversational search mode), and generative features in Discover.** The beta version comes with serious limits, though: it shows impressions only, no clicks, no CTR, no queries. This guide covers where to find the report, how to segment it, and — most importantly — how to read those impressions correctly: as a signal of presence in AI features, not as traffic.

## What Google launched

The **Generative AI features** report has been available in Search Console since June 3, 2026 (Beta), and as of publication it is still rolling out to a subset of sites (Google says access is being granted gradually). Not every Search Console property has it yet. It has two separate views:

- **Search** — AI Overviews and AI Mode in Search.
- **Discover** — generative AI features in Discover.

<Insight tone="key">
Before this report, there was no official Google data source for how often a site shows up in its AI features. Measurement leaned on estimates, a [manual AI visibility test](/blog/ai-visibility-test/), and indirect signals. ## The critical limitation: no clicks

The most important thing to know about this report: **version 1 tracks impressions only.** No clicks, no click-through rate (CTR), no position, no queries.

Google doesn't publish clicks, CTR, position, or queries in this version of the report. In practice, that means you can't read traffic or post-click performance out of it. One likely explanation is that people interact with AI answers differently — those answers often resolve the question right in the interface, so a click isn't necessarily the main outcome of the interaction. We cover that effect in detail in the article on the [zero-click effect](/blog/zero-click-and-ai-overviews/).

<Insight tone="warning">
Don't read AI impressions as traffic. A high impression count in AI Overviews doesn't mean a high visitor count. It means your URLs are showing up often in AI features — which is a signal of presence, not traffic.
</Insight>

## How to read impressions correctly

The right way to read an impressions-only report is to treat impressions as a **signal of presence** — a signal that your URL is showing up in Google's AI features — not as a traffic metric. They can be a useful signal that your content is relevant inside AI features, but they aren't direct proof of quality, traffic, or business value.

## The most common mistakes

    High AI impressions don't mean high traffic. The report doesn't show clicks. Treat impressions as traffic and you'll overestimate what AI visibility is actually worth.

  <Mistake number="02" title="Relying on GSC alone">
    The report covers Google AI only. ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Claude aren't in it. Measure only in Search Console and you're seeing one slice of the AI ecosystem.

## Key takeaways

- **Google launched the Generative AI features report (Beta)** in Search Console on June 3, 2026 — the first official data on Google AI visibility.
- **It shows impressions, pages, countries, devices, and dates** — across two views (Search and Discover).
- **It doesn't show clicks, CTR, position, or queries** — version 1 tracks impressions only.
- **Read impressions as a signal of presence in Google's AI features**, not as traffic; compare them with organic clicks.
- **The report covers Google AI only** — for ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Claude you need a manual test.
- **Supplement GSC** with GA4, UTM, and a manual test — it's one layer, not the full picture.
