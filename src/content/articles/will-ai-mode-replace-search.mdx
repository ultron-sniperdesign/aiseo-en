**Google AI Mode is a conversational search mode that runs on the same index as regular results, not alongside it.** So the question of replacement is really a question about the interface and the click, not about search disappearing. And how many queries are shifting today can't be measured reliably.

This piece closes the AI Mode series, and it's the least rewarding of the lot: the honest answer to the question in the title is "that isn't a question you can ask that way." Which is more useful than it sounds.

## Why the question is framed wrong

It sounds like two systems squaring off: old search and new AI. But according to Google's documentation, AI Mode doesn't draw on a separate index — for a page to appear there, it has to be **indexed and eligible for snippets**, exactly what it needs for regular results. That Google states no special requirements and no separate markup is covered in [how to appear in AI Mode](/blog/how-to-appear-in-ai-mode/).

In other words: it's **a different interface over the same index**. What changes is the way the answer gets served, and what that means for the click — not where the answers come from.

## What Google says — and what it doesn't

Google describes its AI features as an addition: AI Overviews, it says, are meant to add value beyond what a user already gets from Search. Worth saying up front that this is a vendor statement about its own product — authoritative for describing intent, not an independent measurement of impact.

What's more interesting is reading what's claimed and what isn't.

This isn't an accusation, just a suggestion to read closely. With the claim about click quality, it's simply worth noticing that it talks about quality, not count.

## What usually gets offered as proof

When someone claims AI Mode is taking over search, they usually back it with rising search interest in the term. Interest in "AI Mode" really is growing.

<Insight tone="key">
But search demand for a term measures **interest in the word**, not how far the technology has spread or whether behavior has moved. When people are searching in numbers for *what AI Mode is*, that suggests they don't know it yet. Adoption would show up as a share of sessions or queries — and that isn't publicly available.
</Insight>

The second problem is that "AI" gets treated as one thing in claims like that. Yet AI Overviews and AI Mode diverge sharply in what they cite: in a US Ahrefs analysis from September 2025, their cited URLs overlapped by 13.7 percent. More in [AI Mode vs. AI Overviews](/blog/ai-mode-vs-ai-overviews/).

## What we know from our own data

A caveat first: the numbers below cover **visits from external AI assistants**, not AI Mode — so they aren't a measurement of substitution inside Search. On our own store we measured **1,867 visits** from AI tools over twelve months, 90 percent of them sent by ChatGPT. Their conversion rate was 3.5 percent against 0.88 percent from Google organic.

Small volume, higher-quality visit. But it's one store and the starting point before any deliberate optimization — an illustration, not proof about the market. And above all: even these numbers don't tell us how much was lost elsewhere. The data is in the [case study](/blog/case-study-ai-traffic/).

<Insight tone="warning">
Substitution would show up in a comparison of how many queries ended in AI Mode instead of the regular results. But AI Mode can't be isolated from commonly available data — Search Console lumps it in with AI Overviews. Why that is gets covered in [measuring AI Mode](/blog/measuring-ai-mode-limits/). **So nobody today has anything to back a claim about the scale of the replacement.**
</Insight>

## What to do about it in practice

The practical takeaway is boring, and that's a good thing: **most of the work isn't duplicated.** A page has to be findable, readable, and verifiable — whether a person or a model is reading it. That AEO and GEO still rest on the same foundation is covered in [AEO and GEO = SEO](/blog/aeo-geo-is-still-seo/).

What's worth tracking isn't a forecast but your own numbers: the share of visits from assistants, their quality, and how aggregate impressions move over time. For what's happening to clicks, see [zero-click and AI Overviews](/blog/zero-click-and-ai-overviews/).

## Where this article stops

**It doesn't predict.** It doesn't say how this turns out. It says what can and can't be backed up today.

**It has no representative data on how far AI Mode has spread.** The rollout is gradual and public figures aren't available; our own measurement on a single store is an illustration, not a market sample.

**It works with vendor statements too.** Google's claims are labeled as Google's in the text — they describe intent, not independent impact.

## What to take away

According to Google's documentation, AI Mode doesn't draw on a separate index but on the same foundation as regular results. What changes is the interface and the role of the click, not the source of the answers. Google describes its AI features as an addition, and when it talks about clicks it talks about quality, not volume.

How much it's actually taking over, nobody can back up today, because the individual surfaces can't be separated out of the available data. That isn't a reason to do nothing. It's a reason not to make big decisions on numbers that measure something other than what's claimed.
