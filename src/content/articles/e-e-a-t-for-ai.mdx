## E-E-A-T for AI: why it matters more than it used to

**E-E-A-T is a concept from Google's Search Quality Rater Guidelines used to judge content quality — experience, expertise, authoritativeness, and trust.** Google also describes it in its documentation on helpful, reliable content. It isn't a direct ranking factor, but a framework of signals that helps AI systems and Google estimate whether a source is worth trusting. And with the web flooded by AI-generated content, those trust signals may be one of the key ways to tell content backed by real practice apart from interchangeable copy in 2026.

This analysis covers what the four letters mean, why AI is the reason a second E showed up, which pillar matters most, and how to demonstrate trust without a big brand behind you. It adds a trust layer to [content for the AI era](/blog/ai-seo-content/) and an authority angle to [brand mentions and off-page for AI](/blog/ai-brand-mentions/).

<Insight tone="accent" icon="!" label="The takeaway">
  AI can write expert-sounding copy, but it can't have real experience. Demonstrated human experience and trust are what set your content apart from interchangeable generated content.
</Insight>

E-E-A-T has four pillars. The first three are the means; the last one is the goal:

## Why Google added the second E just as AI took off

Google added **Experience** in December 2022, shortly after ChatGPT launched:

<Insight tone="seo" icon="i" label="Experience as a defense against generic AI content">
  AI can produce expert-sounding copy and synthesize information, but it has no practice or first-hand experience of its own. Shifting the emphasis toward Experience makes sense at a time when the volume of generated content is growing fast — even if Google never officially confirmed it as a response to ChatGPT.
</Insight>

You can spot Experience in content by its specific operational detail: which tools you used, which campaigns ran, what the numbers came out to, and what failed. That's exactly the kind of material that only has value when it comes from real practice rather than generic synthesis.

## Trust comes first — and E-E-A-T isn't a score

Two things people get wrong about E-E-A-T more than anything else:

- **Trust sits at the top.** An untrustworthy page has low E-E-A-T no matter how expert or authoritative it otherwise is. Experience, expertise, and authority exist to back that trust up.
- **E-E-A-T is not a switch or a score.** Google publishes no standalone E-E-A-T score — it isn't a single direct ranking factor, but a quality concept that raters use to judge results. Google uses those ratings to evaluate changes to its systems, not as a direct score for a specific page. Don't go looking for a quick lever — this is about quality over the long haul.

## E-E-A-T when you're not a big brand

Trust doesn't start with size, it starts with what you can back up — so a small site has plenty to work with:

- Put a **real author's name** on your content, with documented credentials, and connect that identity across profiles.
- Build on **your own experience and data** that nobody else has.
- Be **transparent** — contact page, about page, sources behind your claims.
- Build authority gradually through [mentions and citations](/blog/ai-brand-mentions/) and [regular content updates](/blog/content-updates-for-ai/).
