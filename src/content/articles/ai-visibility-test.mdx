**An AI visibility test is a manual check on whether and where your brand shows up in the answers from tools like ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Google AI Overviews.** You build 15 to 20 queries that real customers ask, run them across the major AI platforms, and record for each one whether you show up, where, and who is there instead of you. The basic version takes about an hour and no paid tool. This guide walks through the specific process and the common mistakes to avoid.

Treat the result as a directional snapshot for one language, one market, and one moment — not as a definitive measurement of every AI answer.

## Why test your AI visibility

AI visibility is a new discipline, and **the first step is finding out where you stand** — otherwise you're guessing. Some industry analyses in B2B suggest that a large share of brands are functionally invisible to AI answers, but the results vary widely by market and methodology, so treat them as directional.

The good news: **a manual test is cheap and fast.** You don't need a tool, just an hour and a structure. Paid tools only start to make sense for ongoing monitoring, not for a first read.

<Insight tone="aio">
Without a test you're measuring by gut feel. Specific queries run across platforms tell you within an hour whether AI knows about you, what it says about you, and who is showing up instead of you.
</Insight>

## Step 1: Build the right queries

The foundation of the test is **15 to 20 queries** that mirror how your customers actually ask. Split them into four types:

<Checklist title="Four query types for an AI visibility test" items={[
  { title: "Branded queries", desc: "“What is [brand],” “[brand] reviews,” “experience with [brand].” These check whether AI has the right information about you." },
  { title: "Category queries", desc: "“Best [product] in [your city],” “[industry] companies near me.” The most important type — this is where people look for you without knowing your brand." },
  { title: "Problem-solution queries", desc: "“How do I fix [problem],” “what can replace [X].” These show whether AI suggests you as a solution." },
  { title: "Comparison queries", desc: "“[Brand] versus [competitor],” “alternatives to [X].” These show where you stand against the competition." },
]} />

<Insight tone="warn">
The most common mistake is testing branded queries only. You already know your own brand — the point of the test is to find out whether AI suggests you on category and problem queries, where people are looking for you without knowing your name.
</Insight>

## Step 2: Test across platforms

Every AI platform cites differently, so testing on one isn't enough. Three is the minimum.

**Important: test logged out or in a private window.** A signed-in account can shift the results through history, memory, or personalization signals. A private window is closer to what an unfamiliar customer sees. Test in your target language and market, and with Google AI Overviews, watch whether an overview appears for that query type at all.

If you **don't show up at all**, work through the causes from the most technical down:

1. **Are you restricting search crawlers?** Check your robots.txt and any site protection (the Block AI Bots toggle in Cloudflare, for example). Bear in mind, though, that blocking alone isn't the only reason you might not appear in AI answers.
2. **Do you even have content on the question?** AI can't cite what doesn't exist.
3. **Is the brand well known enough?** Few mentions off your own site means a weak signal.
4. **Who is there instead of you, and why?** Analyze the competitors showing up.

## The most common testing mistakes

<MistakeGrid>
  <Mistake num="01" title="One query, one time">
    AI answers aren't the same every time. A single query run once tells you nothing — you need more queries, and each of them repeated. Without repetition you're testing randomness.
  </Mistake>
  <Mistake num="02" title="Branded queries only">
    You already know your own brand. The point of the test is the category and problem queries, where people look for you without knowing your name. Branded queries alone give you a false positive.
  </Mistake>
  <Mistake num="03" title="ChatGPT only">
    Every platform cites differently. Testing on ChatGPT alone will miss the fact that Perplexity or Google AI Overviews might not mention you at all (or the other way around). Test at least three.
  </Mistake>
  <Mistake num="04" title="A signed-in account with history">
    A signed-in account can shift the results through history, memory, or other personalization signals. A signed-in test shows you a picture shaped by your own past activity. Test logged out or in a private window.
  </Mistake>
</MistakeGrid>

## Key takeaways

- **A manual AI visibility test** takes about an hour in its basic form, and it's free.
- **15 to 20 queries** across four types (branded, category, problem-solution, comparison); the category ones matter most.
- **Three platforms at a minimum** — ChatGPT, Perplexity, Google AI Overviews.
- **Repeat three to five times**, because AI answers aren't the same every time, and record appearance, position, competitors, and sources.
- **Test logged out** for an objective picture.
- **An automated tool** makes sense for ongoing monitoring, not for a first audit.
